Honest, Upfront Pricing

Nobody likes a surprise on the invoice. That's why Santa Fe HVAC prices every Santa Fe job upfront, after a full diagnostic that looks at the equipment's age, fuel type, and tonnage, refrigerant levels, coil and heat exchanger condition, and the ductwork feeding each room. You'll know the problem, what the fix involves, and what it costs before you say yes.

The problem with HVAC failures is that many of them develop where you can't see them. A pinhole leak in the evaporator coil slowly drains refrigerant, the blower bearings wear down gradually, and a weak capacitor lasts right up until the first hot afternoon, when the compressor won't start. If your bills are climbing, airflow drops, or ice forms on the lines, have the system checked before a repair turns into a replacement.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Plenty of Santa Fe homeowners ask us to evaluate an aging system before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical AC or furnace lifespan of 12 to 18 years, replacing on your own schedule beats a rushed swap in the middle of a heat wave. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your equipment has left.

Maintenance Plans

Maintenance Plans for Santa Fe Homes

The best repair is the one you never need. That's the idea behind our seasonal plans. Twice a year, we clean coils and burners, check the refrigerant charge, test the electrical components, inspect the heat exchanger, flush the condensate drain, and swap in a new filter, all tailored to the equipment in your home.

Many homes do best with two visits a year: a springtime tune-up for cooling and a fall tune-up for heating. The schedule flexes with the age of your equipment, how hard your household works it, and local conditions like dust and pollen, which coat condenser coils and push up energy use fast.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a regular maintenance schedule cuts operating costs. A clean coil transfers heat far more efficiently, a properly charged system stops overworking the compressor, and a fresh filter protects the blower motor. Most families find that a maintenance plan costs far less than replacing an HVAC system even one year early.

Right-Sized Systems

The Right Size System for Your Home

An undersized system runs nonstop and never catches up on the hottest days, while an oversized one short-cycles, wastes energy, and leaves the house humid. Santa Fe HVAC sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual home. That means a proper load calculation based on square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct layout, rather than simply matching the tonnage of the old unit. For ductless mini-splits, it means matching head capacity to each zone so every room holds its setpoint.

Picking a new system comes down to more than a SEER2 number. Our technician will compare furnace-and-AC setups with heat pumps, and ducted systems with ductless mini-splits, walking through operating costs, electrical and venting requirements, and the installation itself. Families with a growing household often do best with two-stage or variable-speed equipment, which holds more even temperatures from room to room and uses less energy. The decision is yours, based on your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

The HVAC Repairs Santa Fe Homes Need Most

Homes in Santa Fe run into a familiar set of heating and cooling failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Santa Fe HVAC handles them all: no cooling, air that blows but never gets cold, furnaces that won't ignite or short-cycle, frozen evaporator coils, refrigerant leaks, condensers that hum without starting, thermostats that lose their settings, clogged condensate drains, and mini-splits flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A weak capacitor simply keeps the compressor from starting, but a cracked heat exchanger can leak combustion gases into your living space, and a blocked condensate drain can quietly ruin a ceiling. We match the fix to the failure: capacitors, contactors, and fan motors on cooling systems, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards on furnaces, sealed and repaired ductwork where conditioned air is bleeding into the attic, and refrigerant recovered and recharged after a repaired leak. Whatever you're facing, our team has the parts and experience to handle it.
